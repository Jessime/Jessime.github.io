
The timing on my transition from GeneDx is a little serendipitous so I want to capture the chronology.

I'd known that Keith had ideas about turning CROP (the Invitae interpretation platform) into its own thing since our first conversation at ASHG in 2023. So when he told me that he was leaving GeneDx to do his own thing, it wasn't a surprise, especially after hearing about all of the recent Inflection activity. I told him that I'd be around town if he wanted to talk...The benefits of having moved back to SF...

He did want to talk, and we ended up staying in semi-weekly contact over the next couple months. It was very early days, but it looked like there were going to be cofounders and VC funds, provided by Sean. I'm oversimplifying, but the point is that the inital structure was already in place. The conversations we were having were about me being a "Head of AI" or something like that. But I made it clear that from my side, I'd be interested in being a founder if that were possible.

These conversations happened to line up with my 3-week trip to Korea with Erin. Keith and I stayed in touch during the trip, and Jeanne went ahead and committed to joining, even with all of the ambiguity. When the trip ended, I technically got back on a Thursday, and I'd told my boss that I was planning on working that Friday. My assumption was I'd catch up on all the Teams chatter and emails and whatnot.

But by the time Friday morning rolled around, I _fell asleep_ at 6am, after only a couple hours of rough sleep. I slept uninterruped until 6pm on Friday. When I checked my phone, I'd missed an 11am text from my lead asking if I was planning on working today. I told her that I had planned to, but explained the situation and promised to hit the ground running on Monday.

She said that sounded good and that she'd move our 1:1 to 9am Monday.

When I called into our 1:1, the first person I saw on the call was our HR rep. Welp.

I knew immediately that I was getting laid off. I'd had some warning that layoffs were likely around this time, so it didn't catch me completely by surprise. But, I have to admit, I'm never expecting to get axed.

The call lasted 10 minutes and I let Keith and Jeanne know the update about 10 minutes after that. We'd already been trying to find some time to get together, since Jeanne was in town, so we ended up at Naomi's house that night. There were too many taco's to eat and jokes to crack to talk much about layoffs, but it was a relaxing way to end the day of my first layoff.

The next day I called Keith, he asked me if I still wanted to join, and I said, "How about I start tomorrow?"

So I got laid off on Monday and started my new company on Wednesday.
